FAQ

Honest answers to the questions people actually ask.

Pre-approval, commissions, closing costs, timelines. The questions people are sometimes embarrassed to ask. Here they are.

Do I need to be pre-approved before we start looking at homes?

For serious searches, yes. Pre-approval tells us what you can actually afford, and it means when we find your home, we can put in an offer the same day. In Milwaukee's market right now, the buyers who win are the ones who are ready to move. If you don't have a lender yet, I have a few I trust who work well with first-time buyers.

How much money do I actually need to buy a house?

Less than most people think. Down payments can start as low as 3% for conventional loans and 3.5% for FHA. On a $280,000 home, that's roughly $8,400 to $9,800. On top of that, plan for closing costs (usually 2 to 3% of the purchase price) and inspection fees. We'll walk through your specific numbers on our first call.

How does a buyer's agent get paid?

Traditionally, the seller pays both agents' commissions out of the sale proceeds, so as a buyer you don't write me a check. Recent rule changes mean this is now negotiated up front rather than assumed, and we'll go over the specifics before any showings so there are no surprises.

What's earnest money and how much is typical?

Earnest money is your good-faith deposit when you make an offer, showing you're serious. It sits in escrow and gets applied to your down payment at closing. In Milwaukee it's typically 1 to 3% of the purchase price. On a $500,000 home that averages around $7,500. You get it back if the deal falls through for a reason covered by your contract contingencies.

What are closing costs?

Closing costs are the fees to finalize your loan and transfer the property: lender fees, title insurance, appraisal, taxes, and a few smaller line items. For buyers, expect 2 to 3% of the purchase price. For sellers, expect closer to 6 to 8% (that includes the agent commissions). We can sometimes negotiate the seller to help cover buyer closing costs, and we'll talk through that strategy before writing your offer.

How competitive is the Milwaukee market right now?

Extremely competitive right now. Greater Milwaukee listings are averaging 14 days on market, and Bay View, Wauwatosa, Shorewood, and Whitefish Bay move faster than that. Well-priced homes routinely see multiple offers within days. The way you win as a buyer isn't just price. It's a well-crafted offer with strong terms, and knowing which contingencies to keep and which to soften. That's most of my job.

How long does buying a house take?

From our first meeting to keys in hand, expect 60 to 90 days for most transactions. The house-hunt itself can be anywhere from a week to a couple months depending on your criteria and the market. Once we're under contract, closing typically takes 30 days. Cash offers can close faster.

Should I get a home inspection?

Almost always yes. An inspection costs roughly $400 to $600 and gives you a professional evaluation of the foundation, roof, plumbing, electrical, HVAC, and everything in between. In a competitive offer, some buyers waive the inspection contingency to be more attractive, but we'd talk through the risks before doing that on any given house.

How much commission do you charge to sell my home?

Commissions are negotiable and depend on the property, timeline, and marketing scope. I'll walk through my structure openly on our first call, along with exactly what you're getting: professional photography, staging guidance, MLS syndication, social reach to 60,000+ followers, and hands-on offer negotiation. No surprises, and no smoke.

Do I need to fix things or stage my home before listing?

Not everything. On our walkthrough I'll tell you what actually moves the needle (paint colors, decluttering, small repairs) and what's not worth the time or money. My rule: only spend money on prep if it's very likely to come back to you at closing. Staging can be as simple as rearranging what you already own, or as involved as bringing in furniture, depending on the home.

How long will my house take to sell?

Greater Milwaukee listings average 14 days on market right now, and typically drop about 5% in price before they sell. My listings average 3 days on market and typically sell with no price cut at all, at 108% of asking price. That's not luck. It's pricing based on real comps, prep that moves the needle, staging that photographs well, and reaching the right buyers before the sign even goes up. On a $350,000 home, the difference between my 108% and the 101% market average is roughly $24,500 in your pocket. (Source: Metro MLS.)

What if I'm not sure I'm actually ready?

That's most people, honestly. The questionnaire and first call are zero pressure. Sometimes the answer is "you're ready, let's go," sometimes it's "let's revisit in six months and here's what to work on in the meantime." Either way you leave the call with clarity, not a sales pitch.
