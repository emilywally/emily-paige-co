Can I get down payment help as a first time buyer in Milwaukee?

Yes, and it is real money. Each program comes with its own rules, and the rules decide which one fits you.

Yes. If you are buying in the City of Milwaukee and your household income fits the limits, the city’s Home Down Payment Assistance Program offers a grant of $5,000, or $7,000 in parts of the city, and WHEDA, the state housing agency, offers help of up to 6% of the price through its home loans. Most first time buyers I meet have heard of neither, or have heard a little about both and are not sure what is true.

Here is what each program is, who it is for, what you pay back, and what I would ask before you count on any of it.

What down payment help is there in Milwaukee?

Start with two places: the City of Milwaukee and WHEDA (the Wisconsin Housing and Economic Development Authority). The city program is a grant you keep as long as you live in the home. WHEDA’s help is a second loan that sits next to your mortgage. Some lenders and credit unions run their own programs too, and those change often enough that your lender is the best place to hear about them.

One thing to know up front: assistance programs have limited money and their rules get updated, sometimes more than once a year. Everything below is current as of the dates in the sources at the end. Confirm the details with your lender before you build a plan around them.

How does the City of Milwaukee down payment grant work?

Here is how the program reads in the city’s guidelines, revised June 1, 2026:

  • Amount: $5,000 for a home anywhere in the City of Milwaukee, or $7,000 if the home is inside the city’s Community Development Block Grant area. The city has an address lookup that tells you which one applies.
  • What it pays for: your down payment and closing costs.
  • Income: total household income at or below 80% of the area median. For 2026 that is $60,700 for a household of one and $69,400 for a household of two.
  • Who can apply: buyers who have not owned a home in the last three years and who already live in the City of Milwaukee.
  • Homebuyer class: 8 hours of HUD approved homebuyer counseling.
  • Living there: the home has to be where you live. The grant is forgiven once you have lived there five years, so read the repayment terms closely if you think you might move sooner.

If any part of that fits you, book the homebuyer class first. It is required, it is the step people tend to leave for last, and it teaches you things you will use for the rest of the purchase.

What is WHEDA down payment assistance?

WHEDA works through approved lenders. You take a WHEDA first mortgage, and the help comes as a second, smaller loan that closes at the same time. There are two versions to ask about:

  • Easy Close DPA: up to 6% of the purchase price, repaid over 10 years at the same rate as your first mortgage. It is a loan, so it adds a monthly payment.
  • Capital Access DPA: $7,500 at 0% interest with no monthly payment. You repay it when you sell, refinance or pay off the mortgage. WHEDA brought it back on a limited basis on January 15, 2026, with a set amount of money, so ask whether any is left.

To put Easy Close in real numbers: on a $250,000 home, 6% is $15,000. If your first mortgage rate matched the 7.11% national average in Freddie Mac’s survey for the week of November 12, 2026, that $15,000 would cost about $175 a month for ten years on top of your mortgage payment. WHEDA sets its own rates, so treat that as an illustration, not a quote.

WHEDA also sets income and purchase price limits by county, and they are not the same as the city’s limits. A WHEDA lender can tell you in one conversation whether you fit.

Can I combine the city grant with WHEDA help?

Sometimes. Whether two programs can be stacked depends on the loan and the lender, and each program has its own paperwork and timeline. Ask the question directly, early, and in writing: which of these can I use together on this loan, and what does each one do to my monthly payment and my cash to close?

I would also ask what happens if you sell or refinance in a few years. Free money with strings is still a good deal for a lot of people. You just want to know where the strings are.

How much does down payment help save on a Milwaukee home?

More than people expect, especially at the lower end of the market. On a $250,000 home, the 3.5% minimum down payment on an FHA loan is $8,750. The city’s $5,000 grant covers more than half of that, and the $7,000 grant covers most of it. Your closing costs are a separate number, and I break those down in how much cash you need to buy in Milwaukee.

For a sense of scale across the metro, the average sale price in Milwaukee, Waukesha, Ozaukee and Washington counties was $460,243 in October 2026, per the Greater Milwaukee Association of REALTORS® as reported by WisPolitics. Five percent of that average is about $23,000. Prices in the City of Milwaukee itself vary a lot from one street to the next, which is why I would run your own numbers on the homes you are actually looking at.

The tradeoff is simple. Grants lower the cash you need and cost you nothing if you stay. Loans like Easy Close lower the cash you need and raise your monthly payment. Either can be the right call. It depends on which number is harder for you to reach: the money in savings, or the payment each month.

What should I do first?

  • Talk to a lender who offers WHEDA loans and knows the city program, and ask which programs you qualify for today.
  • Book the 8 hour homebuyer class.
  • Ask for the payment and the cash to close with and without assistance, side by side.
  • When you start looking at homes in the city, check each address in the city’s lookup to see whether the $7,000 grant applies.

Quick answers

Is there down payment assistance for first time buyers in Milwaukee?

Yes. The City of Milwaukee offers a grant of $5,000, or $7,000 inside its Community Development Block Grant area, for income eligible buyers who have not owned a home in the last three years. WHEDA, the state housing agency, offers down payment help through approved lenders, including Easy Close DPA of up to 6% of the price.

How much is the City of Milwaukee down payment grant?

$5,000 for a home anywhere in the city, or $7,000 for a home inside the city’s Community Development Block Grant area, per the city’s guidelines revised June 1, 2026. It can be used for down payment and closing costs and is forgiven after five years of living in the home.

Do I have to pay back WHEDA down payment assistance?

Yes. Easy Close DPA is repaid over 10 years at the same rate as your first mortgage. Capital Access DPA, when funds are available, is $7,500 at 0% with no monthly payment and is repaid when you sell, refinance or pay off the mortgage.

What counts as a first time buyer for Milwaukee down payment help?

For the City of Milwaukee grant, it means you have not owned a home in the last three years. WHEDA and individual lenders apply their own definitions, so ask your lender how they apply to you.
