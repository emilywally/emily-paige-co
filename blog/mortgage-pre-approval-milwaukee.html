How mortgage pre-approval actually works when you’re buying in Milwaukee

The single most common question I’ve been asked this month, answered end to end, without the jargon.

If I had to name the one thing that separates the buyers who get the house from the buyers who don’t, in this Milwaukee market, it isn’t their budget. It’s whether their financing was sorted before they walked through the door.

This is the topic that has come up most in my conversations over the past few weeks. What is pre-approval, why does everyone act like it’s a big deal, who do I call, and do I need it in hand before I look at houses. So this week, one post walking through the whole thing.

What is a mortgage pre-approval, exactly?

A pre-approval is a lender’s written statement that they have actually looked at your income, your assets, your credit and your debts, and are prepared to lend you a specific amount on specific terms, contingent on the property itself appraising. It comes as a letter, usually a page long, and you attach it to any offer you write.

It is not the same as a pre-qualification, which is a rough back-of-envelope estimate based on numbers you gave a lender over the phone or through a website. Pre-qualification requires nothing more than a conversation. Pre-approval requires documents and a real underwriting review. Listing agents in Milwaukee know the difference. Yours should too.

Why does pre-approval matter more in Milwaukee than in most places?

Because of the numbers, and the numbers here are stark. The Greater Milwaukee Association of REALTORS® June 2026 report puts metro inventory at 3.4 months, but strip out the homes that already have an accepted offer and what a buyer is actually shopping is closer to 1.6 months of supply. Six months is considered balanced. We are nowhere near it.

The average metro home went under contract in 29 days in the second quarter. The average sale price was $483,551, up 5.7% year over year (that’s an average, so it runs higher than the median). The 30-year fixed rate averaged 6.58% the week of July 23, per Freddie Mac, so payments are not forgiving.

In a market like this, the house you love shows up on Tuesday and needs an offer by Thursday. Without a pre-approval, you cannot write that offer credibly. In Wauwatosa, Shorewood, Bay View, I have watched pre-approved buyers beat higher offers from buyers still gathering paperwork. Sellers and their agents want certainty. Pre-approval is how you signal it.

What do I need to bring to a lender?

Every lender’s intake list looks a little different, but the core is consistent. Two most recent pay stubs. W-2s and tax returns for the last two years. Two most recent bank statements for every account you might draw from. A photo ID. A written explanation of any recent large deposits, so nothing looks like undisclosed borrowing. Your Social Security number, so they can pull credit.

If you own other property, add the mortgage statement, tax bill, and insurance declaration for each. Student loans in deferment or forbearance count too, based on what the payment will be when it resumes.

What if my income is complicated?

Almost every buyer I work with thinks their situation is the weird one. Few actually are. But some do need a lender who handles the nuance.

If you’re self-employed or 1099, most lenders average your last two years of adjusted gross income from your tax returns. The deductions that saved you at tax time may cost you in qualifying income, and your first two full years of self-employment matter more than any single strong month.

If a chunk of your income comes through Venmo, Cash App, or Zelle and isn’t reported, it does not exist for the purposes of qualifying, no matter how consistent it is. If you have a rental property, your lender can typically count 75% of the rent as offsetting income against that property’s mortgage, once they see a lease and a couple of months of deposits. Talk to a lender early if any of this applies. There is almost always a path, but it takes more time.

How long does pre-approval take, and how long does it last?

For a straightforward W-2 borrower with clean credit, most Milwaukee lenders can turn a pre-approval around in one to three business days once they have your documents in hand. More complex files can take a week or two. The letter itself is typically good for 60 to 90 days before the lender wants updated pay stubs and a fresh credit pull.

If you got pre-approved in April, took the summer off to travel, and came back in September, expect to refresh before you write anything.

Should I talk to a lender or an agent first?

Honestly, in whichever order feels less intimidating. But do both before you fall in love with a house, not after. If you don’t have a lender you trust, tell me. I have three I regularly send first-time buyers to, and I picked them because they answer their phones on Saturday mornings, they explain things without condescension, and they don’t oversell you. If you already have a bank or a credit union you like, start there. Just start.
