How much house can I afford in Milwaukee?

The bank gives you a ceiling. The number you can live with is usually lower, and property taxes are the part people miss.

Affordability is the worry I hear more than any other right now, and it usually arrives as a question about the bank. How much will they approve me for? That is a fair place to start. It is a bad place to stop.

A pre-approval tells you the most a lender is willing to lend. It does not know that you like to travel, that daycare starts next fall, or what else you would like your paycheck to do. You know those things. So the number that matters is the one you build yourself, starting from a monthly payment you are comfortable with and working backward to a price.

How much house can I afford on my income?

The lender’s answer comes from three things: what you earn, what you already owe each month, and your credit. I walk through how that works in my post on getting pre-approved in Milwaukee. Conventional, FHA and VA loans each count things a little differently, and which one fits you is a question for a lender, not a blog post.

The same goes for the situations that come up in almost every consult: a new job that pays on commission, student loans, a partner who is still in school or earns less. None of those is a dealbreaker on its own. All of them change the math, and a lender would much rather hear about them on day one than in week five. If you are thinking about paying down debt first, ask the lender which balance moves your number before you drain savings to clear it. Sometimes it is the car loan. Sometimes it is nothing.

Then comes my answer, which is different. After watching a lot of people through this, I would pick the payment that lets you keep living your life, and shop below your approval if that is where it lands. Nobody has ever told me they regret having money left at the end of the month.

What is the monthly payment on a $300,000 house in Milwaukee?

Take a $300,000 house as the illustration, with 5% down:

  • The loan: $285,000.
  • Principal and interest: about $1,917 a month on a 30-year fixed at 7.11%, the average rate in Freddie Mac’s survey for the week of November 12, 2026.
  • Property taxes: about $520 a month, if the house sits in the City of Milwaukee and is assessed near its price.
  • Homeowners insurance: about $156 a month, the Milwaukee average Insure.com reported for $300,000 of dwelling coverage.

That is roughly $2,593 a month. With less than 20% down on a conventional loan, mortgage insurance comes on top, and your lender can quote it. Insurance is the line I would get a real quote on early, because an older roof or an aging electrical panel can move it more than people expect.

A year earlier, Freddie Mac’s average was 6.24%, and the same loan would have cost about $164 less each month. I do not predict rates. I just like people to know which part of the payment moves.

How much are property taxes in Milwaukee County?

This is the part buyers moving here from out of state miss, and local buyers tend to underestimate. The same price can carry a very different tax bill a few miles apart.

Here are 2025 rates per $1,000 of assessed value, from the Milwaukee County Treasurer’s comparison of municipal rates: Whitefish Bay $14.86, Wauwatosa $17.80, the City of Milwaukee $20.80, West Allis $22.14. On a home assessed at $300,000, that is about $372 a month at the low end of that list and $554 at the high end. Same price, roughly $180 a month apart.

Two caveats keep this honest. Each community assesses on its own schedule, so the rate alone does not tell you the bill. And bills move: the City of Milwaukee’s 2025 revaluation raised residential values 14.39%, according to the city assessor’s office as reported by Milwaukee Neighborhood News Service in December 2025. New bills for this year go out in December.

So for any house you are serious about, ask for the most recent tax bill and plug that into your payment, not an estimate. Taxes are one line in a much bigger decision, and I would never suggest choosing where to live by mill rate. I would suggest knowing it before you fall for the kitchen.

What should I leave room for after the mortgage?

A lot of the housing around here is older: bungalows, duplexes, Cape Cods with a finished attic that someone finished themselves in 1974. Those homes are a big part of why people love living here. They also tend to come with a furnace that has opinions.

The payment you can just barely make is the one that leaves nothing for any of that. It also leaves nothing for the paint color that makes the place feel like yours, the rug for the front room, or having people over once you finally have the room to do it. A house is supposed to make your life bigger. Leave space in the budget for that to happen.

The market matters here too. The four-county metro had 3.4 months of supply in September 2026, per the Greater Milwaukee Association of REALTORS® report released October 13, 2026, well short of the six months considered balanced. The right house rarely waits while you run the numbers, which is the best argument I know for running them first.

Where should I start?

Pick your comfortable monthly number. Get pre-approved, and ask your lender to show you the payment at a few different prices, not just the maximum. Then add the cash side, which I break down in how much cash you need to buy in Milwaukee.

If you would like a second set of eyes on any of it, reach out. Bring the payment you have in mind and a few listings you have been circling, and we can look at what those houses really cost to live in. I’d love to hear about it.

Quick answers

How much house can I afford in Milwaukee?

A lender sets the maximum from your income, monthly debts and credit. The better number to plan around is the monthly payment you are comfortable with, worked backward to a price. Shopping below your approval is common and often wise.

What is the monthly payment on a $300,000 house in Milwaukee?

With 5% down, principal and interest on a 30-year fixed loan at 7.11% (the Freddie Mac average for the week of November 12, 2026) is about $1,917 a month. City of Milwaukee property taxes add about $520 a month if the home is assessed near its price, and homeowners insurance averages about $156 a month in Milwaukee for $300,000 of dwelling coverage, per Insure.com. That is roughly $2,593 a month before any mortgage insurance.

How much are property taxes in Milwaukee County?

It depends on the community and the assessed value. 2025 rates per $1,000 of assessed value included $14.86 in Whitefish Bay, $17.80 in Wauwatosa, $20.80 in the City of Milwaukee and $22.14 in West Allis, per the Milwaukee County Treasurer. Ask for the most recent tax bill on any home you are considering.
