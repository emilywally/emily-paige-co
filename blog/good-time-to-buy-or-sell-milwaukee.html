Is now a good time to buy or sell in Milwaukee?

I get some version of this question every week. Here’s the real answer, numbers included.

I get some version of this question almost every week, from buyers and sellers both. Usually it arrives sideways — at a showing, in a text, at the end of a call about something else. So… is now a good time?

Most of the time the honest answer is yes, but not for the reason you’re asking. Let me show you what I mean.

What do the current numbers actually say?

Here’s where things stood as of the most recent reporting, from the Wisconsin REALTORS® Association June 2026 report, released July 23:

The statewide median sale price hit $360,000, up 5.9% from a year ago. Existing home sales were also up 5.9% year over year. Available supply held at 4.2 months — exactly where it was a year ago, and still well under the six months that would signal a balanced market. New listings rose 4.8%.

On financing: the 30-year fixed mortgage averaged 6.58% the week of July 23, per Freddie Mac. That’s down from 6.74% at this point last year. Not dramatic relief. But it is movement in the right direction, and it is not the 8% some people still have in their heads.

One more figure, and it’s the one I keep thinking about. In June 2021, 68.9% of Wisconsin listings were priced under $350,000. This June, that share was 46.2%. In five years, the affordable half of the market quietly became the smaller half.

A note on all of this: those are statewide Wisconsin figures, because that’s the data that gets published on a clean monthly cycle. Which brings me to the part that matters more.

Why the metro average probably doesn’t describe your house

A statewide median blends Milwaukee with Madison with Door County with the Northwoods. Even a Milwaukee-metro average blends Whitefish Bay with Cudahy, and the Third Ward with West Allis. Those are not the same market. They are barely the same conversation.

Bay View moves differently than Wauwatosa. Riverwest moves differently than Shorewood. A well-prepped bungalow at the right price point can see real competition in a week where a similar house two neighborhoods over sits for a month. Averages are useful for headlines and almost useless for decisions.

So when someone asks me if it’s a good time, what I actually need to know is: which street, which price band, and what condition.

Is now a good time to sell a house in Milwaukee?

If you’re genuinely ready — yes. At 4.2 months of supply, a correctly priced and properly prepared home is still meeting more demand than competition. Prices are up, not down. That’s a reasonable position to sell from.

The caveat is that “correctly priced and properly prepared” is doing enormous work in that sentence. Buyers in this market are paying real money at real interest rates, and they are unforgiving about homes that are priced on hope. Overpricing in a 4.2-month market doesn’t get you a slow sale. It gets you a stale listing and a price cut you can’t take back.

Is now a good time to buy a house in Milwaukee?

Also yes — with clear eyes. Rates near 6.5% mean your monthly payment is what it is, and no amount of optimism changes that math. Run the real number before you fall in love with anything.

But here’s what I’d point out: a lot of buyers are sitting out right now, waiting for a signal. That hesitation is competition you’re not facing. Inventory is up modestly. You have more room to inspect carefully, negotiate, and walk away than buyers had two or three years ago — and that room has real value that never shows up in a headline.

What matters more than timing the market?

This is the actual answer to the question.

Nobody times this well. Not me, not the economists, not the person on the podcast. What I’ve watched sort people into “glad we did it” and “wish we’d waited” has almost never been the month they signed. It’s been three things:

Whether their timeline was real. A growing family, a job change, a house that stopped fitting, downsizing after the kids left — those needs don’t pause for ideal conditions, and waiting them out has its own cost.

Whether they knew their actual numbers. Not the national average. Their payment, their equity, their cushion, their walk-away point.

Whether they were prepared when it mattered. The people who do well aren’t the ones who guessed right about rates. They’re the ones who were ready to move decisively when the right house showed up.

So — should you move this year?

If you’ve been waiting for a headline to tell you it’s safe, I’ll save you the wait: it isn’t coming. The market is going to keep doing what it does. The only question that’s ever really been on the table is whether your life is ready to move alongside it.

If you’re thinking it through, start with the questionnaire — buyer or seller, about five minutes. It tells me your neighborhood, your price range, and your timeline, so our first conversation starts somewhere useful instead of at the beginning. Or just reach out and ask me directly.
