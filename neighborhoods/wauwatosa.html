Moving to Wauwatosa: what to know before you buy

Wauwatosa (everyone says Tosa) sits just west of Milwaukee, about 7 miles and roughly a 12 minute drive from downtown. It has a walkable village center on the Menomonee River, large parks, a regional medical campus and a wide range of older homes, and it is priced above most of Milwaukee County.

What should I know about moving to Wauwatosa?

Three things. Homes near the Village and the parks cost the most, so your budget will narrow your search more than your wish list will. Many of the homes are older, which means the roof, the boiler and the electrical panel deserve a close look at every showing. And the city is really several districts with different streets, different homes and different prices, so it pays to choose a district before you choose a home.

The rest of this page walks through the streets, parks and prices so you can picture a Tuesday here before you visit.

Where is the center of Wauwatosa?

The Village is the on foot heart of the city. It runs along West State Street and Harwood Avenue beside the Menomonee River, with independent restaurants, cafes and shops within a few blocks of each other. Ristorante Bartolotta dal 1993 is at 7616 W State St, Café Hollander is at 7677 W State St, and Le Rêve Patisserie & Cafe is at 7610 Harwood Ave. If you like to leave the car parked for a Saturday, this is the part of town that makes it easy.

Which Wauwatosa neighborhoods should I look at?

The city is organized loosely around five named districts, and each one shows you something different.

The Village. Older homes on streets close to State Street, Harwood Avenue and the river. Hart Park, at 7300 W Chestnut St, is a short walk or ride away.
East Tosa. The North Avenue corridor on the city's east edge, with bakeries and coffee shops such as Rocket Baby Bakery at 6822 W North Ave.
Midtown. The middle of the city, near the Milwaukee Regional Medical Center at 8701 Watertown Plank Rd, where Froedtert, Children's Wisconsin and the Medical College of Wisconsin share a campus.
The Mayfair area. The west side, around Mayfair Mall at 2500 N Mayfair Rd and Highway 100, with larger lots and bigger colonial style homes.
68th and Wells. A smaller commercial crossroads with its own mix of older homes nearby.

What parks are in Wauwatosa?

Hart Park (19.5 acres, along the Menomonee River) and Hoyt Park (1800 N Swan Blvd, with the TOSA Pool) are the two big ones. Both sit on the Menomonee River Parkway, so you get trails and river access without leaving the city.

How much do homes cost in Wauwatosa?

Zillow's home value index puts a typical Wauwatosa home at about $435,000 as of mid 2026, up roughly 4 percent year over year. Movoto's June 2026 median sale price sits nearby at about $419,900. Prices vary by district: the Village and the streets around it tend to run higher than the western edge of the city.

What are the tradeoffs of living in Wauwatosa?

Wauwatosa is expensive by Milwaukee County standards, and it has been for a while. Inventory is thin, and if you want the walkable village, you will pay for it. In my experience, homes near the Village core often sell over asking with escalation clauses, and the bank's check on the home's value comes in below the offer more often than most first time buyers expect.

The housing is also older. Roofs, boilers and 100 amp electrical panels are the three things that come up most. Budget for the honest condition of the house, not the polished listing photos.
