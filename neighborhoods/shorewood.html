Shorewood, a mile and a half of village between the river and the lake.

A realtor's notes on Shorewood. What it costs, what's there, and what's worth knowing before you buy or sell here.

Shorewood is a small village on Lake Michigan immediately north of Milwaukee's East Side and south of Whitefish Bay, occupying roughly 1.59 square miles of land between the Milwaukee River on the west and the lake on the east. The village recorded 13,859 residents in the 2020 census, and the Village of Shorewood describes it as the densest community in Wisconsin. It was incorporated in August 1900 as the Village of East Milwaukee and renamed Shorewood in 1917.

Shorewood grew as a streetcar suburb, and its commercial center still runs along the intersecting lines of the old routes: Oakland Avenue north-south and Capitol Drive (originally Atwater Road) east-west. The Village describes the community as "walkable, bike-friendly and architecturally diverse." Downer Avenue at the village's southern edge extends a second walkable commercial strip into neighboring Milwaukee.

Housing stock reflects an early 20th century boom, with as many as 200 homes built per year during the 1920s. Notable architects working in the village included Alexander C. Eschweiler, Russell Barr Williamson (16 or more homes designed 1918 to 1929), and Frank Lloyd Wright's American System-Built Homes program. Larger homes and mansions cluster along Lake Drive on the eastern edge.

Public schools are operated by the Shorewood School District, which runs four schools: Shorewood High School, Shorewood Intermediate School, and two elementary schools, Atwater and Lake Bluff. Public lakefront access is anchored by Atwater Park, whose staircase leads down the bluff to a sandy beach. Hubbard Park along the Milwaukee River adds trail and event space via the Oak Leaf Trail corridor.

What Shorewood costs right now

Movoto lists a June 2026 median sale price of about $595,000 for Shorewood. Homes.com shows a similar figure near $600,000, up about 18 percent year over year. Shorewood is one of the higher-priced markets on the north side of Milwaukee, and inventory tends to move quickly.

Who tends to like Shorewood

Not who "should" live here. Just what kind of daily life this place fits.

If you want a walkable downtown with cafes, bars, and a grocery within a few blocks of home, Shorewood's Oakland Avenue corridor concentrates coffee, dining, and daily shopping into a compact main street.
If you want direct Lake Michigan access, Atwater Park's bluff-top green space and staircase down to the beach are inside village boundaries.
If you want older, architecturally varied housing stock, most of the village was built out during the 1920s streetcar-suburb boom and includes homes by regionally noted architects.
If you want a compact village with its own municipal services and school district but Milwaukee at your doorstep, Shorewood sits within about 1.6 square miles yet borders Milwaukee's East Side and is minutes from downtown by car.
