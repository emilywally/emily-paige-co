Neighborhood guide · Written by Emily Walczak

Franklin, room to spread out with a ballpark down the road.

A realtor's notes on Franklin. What it costs, what's there, and what's worth knowing before you buy or sell here.

Franklin is one of the bigger suburbs in Milwaukee County by land area, and it feels that way. Neighborhoods are established but spread out, lots run bigger than what you get closer in, and driving is how most people get where they are going.

What has really changed Franklin in the last decade is Ballpark Commons. It is a mixed-use sports and entertainment district on the east side of the city with a professional baseball stadium (home to the Milwaukee Milkmen and the UWM Panthers), an indoor golf entertainment complex with 57 hitting bays, a drive-in, and restaurants. On a summer weekend it is one of the more active spots on the south side.

Parks and outdoor space are a real part of the pitch here. The Root River runs through the city, and the Milwaukee County Root River Parkway follows it. Oakwood Park has a public golf course inside it. There is more green space on your doorstep in Franklin than in most Milwaukee County cities.

Schools are handled by the Franklin Public School District for most of the city, with pockets in the Oak Creek-Franklin and Whitnall districts. Franklin sits four miles from Mitchell Airport and has direct access to I-94, so getting anywhere in the metro is straightforward.

Franklin is one of the far southwest suburbs I show buyers who want space, quiet, and newer construction. It sits below Greendale and Greenfield, stretches west to New Berlin, and has grown steadily over the last two decades with new subdivisions, big-box retail along 27th, and school districts (Franklin Public and Oak Creek-Franklin) that are why most of my Franklin buyers moved there.

Most Franklin clients are families trading up out of a first house in the city, or relocating professionals who want a new-build in a good school district and do not care about walkability. What Franklin sells is space, updated systems, and predictable subdivision living. That is a real value proposition for a certain buyer.

What I tell people considering Franklin is that you need to like driving. Nothing here is walking distance. If that is a dealbreaker, we should be looking somewhere else. If it is not, Franklin gives you newer housing at a lower price per square foot than the north-shore suburbs.

What Franklin costs right now

Zillow's home value index puts a typical Franklin home at about $451,972 as of mid-2026, up 3.7 percent year over year. Franklin sits at the higher end of the southern Milwaukee County market, reflecting a newer overall housing stock than the inner-ring suburbs.

Who tends to like Franklin

Not who "should" live here. Just what kind of daily life this place fits.

If you want a bigger lot and a suburban setting without a long commute, Franklin has more room than most Milwaukee-adjacent cities.
If you want a live entertainment district without driving into Milwaukee, Ballpark Commons puts baseball, indoor golf, and dining inside the city.
If parks and river-corridor trails matter, the Root River Parkway and Oakwood Park run through Franklin.
If you fly a lot, Mitchell Airport is about four miles away.

The honest tradeoffs

Franklin is a car-required neighborhood. You will drive to groceries, to dinner, to the coffee shop, to anywhere. That is the deal, and it works for a lot of families, but if you have moved from a walkable city neighborhood it can take an adjustment.

Newer subdivisions can also feel same-y from block to block. Cul-de-sacs, similar floor plans, similar exteriors. The character of an old Milwaukee neighborhood is not really the point of Franklin. What is the point is the school district, the yard, the attached garage, and the low-maintenance house that will not need a new sewer lateral in year three.
