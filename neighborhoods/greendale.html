Neighborhood guide · Written by Emily Walczak

Greendale, one of three New Deal towns in the country.

A realtor's notes on Greendale. What it costs, what's there, and what's worth knowing before you buy or sell here.

Greendale is not like most Milwaukee suburbs, and the reason is history. It is one of only three federal Greenbelt Towns built anywhere in the United States (the other two are in Maryland and Ohio), and it was designed in the 1930s as an experiment in New Deal-era planned communities. Streets curve, utilities are buried, the village center is walkable by design, and the whole downtown is a National Historic Landmark.

That planning shows. The village center feels closer to a small town from a movie set than a Milwaukee suburb. Colonial Revival civic buildings, tens of thousands of flowers planted every year, and a real walkable stretch of shops and restaurants.

The most distinctive housing here is the Originals. These are the 366 homes built by the federal government in 1938, concrete-block with clay tile roofs, and most of them have distinctive artistic chimney designs. There is nothing else like them in the metro. Around that original core, standard suburban expansion has filled in.

Schools have a strong reputation. Greendale High has been ranked among the country's better public high schools, and the marching band has been a state champion 18 years running. Southridge Mall and the S 76th retail corridor cover most of the daily errand shopping.

Greendale is a planned village from the 1930s, one of only three Depression-era planned "greenbelt towns" the federal government built, and you can still see the intent everywhere. Curved streets, connected greenspace, a distinctive downtown along Broad Street with covered sidewalks and the original town hall. It looks and feels like nowhere else in the metro.

Most of the Greendale clients I work with are families drawn to the schools and the town center, or long-time Milwaukee locals who grew up visiting and always wanted to live there. The community here is unusually tight because so many households have been in place for generations.

What I tell buyers new to Greendale is that the original village homes have real historic character (and real historic quirks). The newer subdivisions on the edges are a different product entirely. Both are Greendale on the map, but they are two different searches.

What Greendale costs right now

Movoto puts the June 2026 median sale price at about $387,900, and Homes.com shows a similar figure near $392,500, up about 9 percent year over year. Greendale's inventory is unusually distinctive (the Originals are unlike anything else in the Milwaukee suburbs), which affects how a specific home prices.

Who tends to like Greendale

Not who "should" live here. Just what kind of daily life this place fits.

If you want a walkable, planned town center with real history, Greendale's village core is a National Historic Landmark district.
If you want a distinctive character house that literally does not exist elsewhere, the 1938 Originals are one of a kind, with concrete-block construction and artistic chimney designs.
If the schools matter to your decision, Greendale High has been ranked among the country's better public high schools.
If you want convenient regional shopping and dining, Southridge Mall and the S 76th Street corridor are inside the village.

The honest tradeoffs

The original village homes are protected by a review board that limits certain exterior changes. That preserves the neighborhood's character but means less flexibility if you want to remodel outside the historic aesthetic. Know what you are signing up for before you write the offer.

Greendale sits further from downtown Milwaukee than the north-shore suburbs, so the daily commute for anyone working downtown or east-side is longer. Retail is decent along Highway 100 but not walkable from most of the neighborhood. It is a suburb, not a village dressed up as one, and buyers looking for city energy will not find it here.
