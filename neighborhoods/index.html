Neighborhoods

Milwaukee, neighborhood by neighborhood.

Working guides to the neighborhoods and suburbs I show and sell in most. Written by a realtor who works across the metro, not a copy-paste of the same page 16 times.

City neighborhoods

Bay View

Bungalows, Kinnickinnic Ave, and a lake at the end of every east-west street.

Riverwest

Where the streets throw their own festivals.

East Side

Milwaukee's original urban neighborhood, running along the river and the lake.

Third Ward

Milwaukee at its most pedestrian: warehouses, cobblestones, condos.

Walker's Point

Warehouse-turned-neighborhood south of the office core.

Near-in suburbs (first ring)

Wauwatosa

A small village center along the Menomonee, west of the city.

Shorewood

Compact, historic, and priced for the schools.

Whitefish Bay

The north-shore family village that runs on schools and the lake.

West Allis

Honest bargain: Greenfield Avenue, old bungalows, the State Fair.

Cudahy

South-shore neighbor to Bay View with Sheridan Park on the lake.

St. Francis

The quieter south-shore option between Bay View and Cudahy.

Middle and outer suburbs

Greendale

A 1930s planned Greenbelt Town, still one of the country's only three.

Greenfield

Middle-ring suburb built around mid-century ranches and easy freeway access.

Franklin

Far southwest suburb: newer builds, bigger lots, drive to everything.

New Berlin

Western suburb: newer homes, top-rated schools, subdivision living.

Oak Creek

The suburb that built itself a town center from scratch (Drexel Town Square).
