West Allis, State Fair and Greenfield Ave.

A realtor's notes on West Allis. What it costs, what's there, and what's worth knowing before you buy or sell here.

West Allis is one of the first Milwaukee suburbs, close in, and it feels close in. It grew up around a big factory in the early 1900s and still carries that legacy in the housing stock. Bungalows, Cape Cods, and ranches on gridded streets. Not much in the way of subdivisions.

Greenfield Avenue is the on-foot spine. It has independent restaurants, breweries, bakeries, and enough personality to feel like a real main street instead of a strip mall. The West Allis Farmers Market runs three days a week in season and is one of the better ones in the metro.

The other headline is State Fair Park. The Wisconsin State Fair happens there every August and takes over the city for eleven days. In the rest of the year, the park hosts the Pettit ice center (one of the only indoor speed-skating ovals in the country and a training home for the U.S. team) and enough of a calendar that there is almost always something going on.

Freeway access is excellent. I-94 and I-894 both cut through the city, so getting downtown or to Mitchell Airport is a straight shot. Prices come in noticeably lower than the north shore suburbs, which is the honest reason a lot of people who work in Milwaukee end up buying here.

West Allis is one of the honest Milwaukee-adjacent bargains that has been quietly moving up. It borders the city on the west, runs along Greenfield Avenue for its main commercial spine, and hosts the Wisconsin State Fair grounds every August. The West Allis buyers I see are usually first-time buyers or investors who priced out of Bay View or Wauwatosa and want the same easy-to-walk streets at a friendlier number.

Housing here is dense, older, and mostly modest. Bungalows and Cape Cods on gridded blocks, worker's cottages, the occasional duplex, and a few pockets of ranches from the 50s and 60s. Not many yards to speak of, driveways can be narrow, but the price point is real.

West Allis is a block-by-block search, more than most of the metro. Two streets over can feel like a different neighborhood. Do not judge the neighborhood by a drive down Greenfield. Get onto the residential blocks north and south of it.

What West Allis costs right now

Redfin puts the February 2025 median sale price at about $255,250, up 6.4 percent year over year. Zillow's home value index shows a similar figure near $260,500 through late 2025. West Allis is one of the more accessible price points inside the immediate Milwaukee ring.

Who tends to like West Allis

Not who "should" live here. Just what kind of daily life this place fits.

If you want an independent-business commercial corridor you can walk, Greenfield Avenue puts breweries, bakeries, and neighborhood restaurants inside a compact stretch.
If you want a shorter freeway trip to downtown Milwaukee, the city sits directly on I-94 and I-894.
If you want a lower price point than the north shore while staying close to the city, West Allis prices consistently below Shorewood and Whitefish Bay.
If you want big annual events on your doorstep, State Fair Park runs the state fair, the Pettit ice center, and events year round inside city limits.

The honest tradeoffs

Aesthetics vary widely block to block. Some pockets are beautifully maintained mid-century blocks with mature trees. Others are tired. Tour a few streets before you commit to a specific pocket.

The older housing stock brings the usual list: older electrical, older sewer laterals, some knob-and-tube still in pockets, plaster walls that hide surprises. On the plus side, the price point almost always leaves room for the updates that come with a hundred-year-old house.
