Lights, Camera, Listed

Your Pre-Photo Prep Guide

Your listing photos are the first showing every buyer attends, so this is where your prep pays off most. It's also the perfect time to start clearing out anything that isn't coming with you to your next home. Once an offer comes in, you may only have 30 days to get the house empty and clean, so use this window wisely!

We'll walk through your home together before photos and go over everything specific to your space. Until then, here is what we have every seller work through.

Outside first

Curb appeal sets the tone before a buyer ever steps inside, so don't underestimate it.

  • Trim bushes, pull weeds, and add a fresh layer of dark mulch
  • In the colder months, keep walkways, steps and the driveway shoveled and salted instead
  • Make sure landscaping isn't blocking your windows or doors
  • Power wash the driveway, sidewalks, porches and patio
  • Clean the windows inside and out
  • Scrub the front door and give it a fresh coat of paint or stain if it's looking worn
  • Clean or replace old patio furniture and cushions so the outdoor space looks intentional

The entry

The lockbox will live on your front door, so this is the first thing buyers experience in person.

  • Make sure it's clean, bright and odor free

Living and dining rooms

  • Clear every surface, including window sills
  • Declutter built-in shelves
  • Remove fake florals and heavy patterned drapes. Affordable white drapes photograph beautifully and make rooms feel larger and brighter

The kitchen

  • Clear the countertops and the top of the refrigerator
  • Make sure the pantry floor is clearly visible
  • Wipe down every appliance

Bathrooms

  • Clear the countertops completely

Bedrooms

  • Clear dresser tops
  • Swap themed or heavily patterned bedding, including kids' bedding, for white quilts
  • Make sure closet floors are clearly visible. Buyers will open every door, so give them something good to see

Throughout the home

  • Hide all power cords, cables and trash cans. If a buyer can see it, so can the camera
  • Remove personal photos, diplomas and personalized certificates where possible. You want buyers to picture themselves in the space, not feel like a guest in yours
  • Check every light bulb. They should all be working and matching
  • Clean all ceiling fans
  • Do a final detail clean before the photographer arrives, wiping down appliances, baseboards and window sills so everything looks sharp up close

The garage

Good news here. Your garage won't be photographed, so it can absolutely be used for storage during this process.

  • Pack items neatly and stack them against the wall to keep the floor as clear as possible
  • Give it a good sweep so it's tidy for showings
  • Keep in mind that when you move out, the garage and any storage sheds will need to be fully emptied

A note on vacant homes

If your home is empty, condition matters even more because there's nothing to draw the eye away from imperfections. Before photo day, do a close pass on:

  • Flooring and baseboards
  • Walls and paint condition
  • Light fixtures and blinds
  • Cabinet condition and hardware
  • Any maintenance items that would catch a buyer's eye
